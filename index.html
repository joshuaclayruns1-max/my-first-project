        Sample training week

        A common shape for 800m runners in season. Adjust it with your coach, and shrink it if you're new or coming back from injury.

        MonEasy run 20–35 min + strides (6x100m)
        TueSpeed endurance: e.g. 4x300m at 800 pace, full rest (4–6 min)
        WedEasy run + strength (squats, lunges, core, calf raises)
        ThuAerobic power: e.g. 5x600m a bit slower than mile pace, 2 min rest, or a 15–20 min tempo
        FriRest or easy shakeout + mobility
        SatRace, or short fast work: e.g. 2x(200m, 200m) fast with walk rest
        SunLonger easy run (40–60 min), relaxed

        What makes you faster indoors

        • Speed endurance. The 800 is mostly about holding speed while tired. Reps of 200–400m at race pace or faster train that.
        • Aerobic base. Most of an 800 runs on aerobic energy. Easy miles still matter, so keep easy days truly easy.
        • Practice on the turns. Indoor tracks are tight (often 200m). Run some reps indoors to learn to lean into turns and hold your lane.
        • Race tactics. Indoors, position matters. Practice a fast first 100m to get a good spot, then relax down the back.
        • Strength twice a week. Stronger legs and core help with turns, kicks, and staying injury-free.
        • Sleep 8+ hours. Most of your adaptation happens while you recover.

        Eating for the 800

        • Carbs fuel hard days. Eat more rice, pasta, oats, potatoes, and fruit on workout and race days.
        • Protein at every meal. Eggs, chicken, Greek yogurt, beans, fish, milk. Spread it through the day.
        • Eat after hard sessions. A snack with carbs and protein within about an hour (chocolate milk works).
        • Iron matters for runners. Lean red meat, beans, spinach, fortified cereal. If you feel tired all the time, ask a doctor to check it.
        • Don't under-eat. Not eating enough slows recovery and raises injury risk. Fuel the training you're doing.
        • Race day: a familiar, light meal 3–4 hours before, and a small snack (banana, toast) about an hour before.

        General guidance, not medical advice. Talk to your coach or a doctor about your own plan.
